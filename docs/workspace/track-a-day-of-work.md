This tutorial follows Sam, a designer on a team, through one working day. Sam works on two projects, *Website* and
*Brochure*. By the end you will have used the Inbox, My tasks, the timer, a hand-logged entry, and the Timesheet.

## Morning: see what is waiting

1. Sam opens **Inbox** in the sidebar. The link shows `2` unread. One item says a colleague assigned Sam a task, and
   the other says a task Sam commented on was moved.
2. Sam clicks the first. It is marked read, and the task opens over the inbox. Sam gives it a due date of Friday and
   closes it. See [Inbox](/help/workspace/inbox).
3. Sam clicks **My tasks**. On the **Upcoming** tab there are five tasks, soonest due first. The new one is among
   them. Sam decides to start on **Update the homepage hero**. See [My tasks](/help/workspace/my-tasks).

## Start the timer

4. Sam clicks the task. Its panel opens on the *Website* board.
5. In the **Time** section, under **Blocked by**, Sam clicks **Start timer**. The button turns red and counts up, and a
   timer with the task's name now shows in the sidebar.
6. Sam works for a while, then switches to *Brochure* pages and back. The timer keeps running through all of it.

## A switch of task

7. An hour and a half later, a colleague asks Sam to check a *Brochure* task. Sam opens it and clicks **Start timer**
   there.
8. Starting this timer stops the first one: the hour and a half is logged on **Update the homepage hero**, and the
   sidebar now shows the *Brochure* task. Sam did not have to stop anything by hand.

## Forgot to start: log by hand

9. At lunch Sam remembers a call this morning, before the timer started. It took 45 minutes and belongs to the *Website*
   task **Plan the homepage content**.
10. Sam opens **Timesheet** in the sidebar. In the form under the grid, Sam picks **Plan the homepage content** under
    *Website* in **Pick a task…**, types `45m`, leaves the day on today, writes **Call with the client** in **What
    was it?**, and clicks **Add time**.
11. The entry appears under **Today**, and the grid gets a row for the task.

## End of day

12. Sam finishes the *Brochure* check and clicks **Stop timer** (the square button) on the sidebar timer. The time is
    logged on the *Brochure* task, on today's date.
13. In the **Timesheet**, the grid now shows three rows with Today's column filled in, and a total at the top such as
    `4h 5m this week`. Under **Today** are the three entries, newest first.
14. One entry is wrong: Sam typed `45m` for something that took `30m`. Sam hovers it and clicks the trash button, then
    adds it again as `30m`. There is no edit, so a correction is a delete and a new entry. See
    [Start a timer and log time](/help/workspace/log-time).

## What Sam's team sees

15. The project lead opens the *Website* board and clicks the **Time** tab. In **By person**, a row for Sam shows
    today's time on this project. In **By task**, the two tasks Sam worked on show their totals. The *Brochure* time is
    on the *Brochure* board's own tab.
16. Nobody saw the running timer, and nobody sees Sam's **Timesheet** page. They see the entries that are on their own
    project's tasks and Time tab, with Sam's name. See [The Timesheet and a project's Time tab](/help/workspace/timesheet).

## What to take from it

- One timer at a time: starting a second one logs the first for you.
- Anything you forgot can be added later, to any day.
- Entries cannot be edited. Delete and re-enter.
- A timer you forget to stop keeps counting. When you stop it, the time is logged on the day you stop it, and no
  entry is longer than 24 hours. If you left it running overnight, stop it, delete the entry, and log the time you
  really worked by hand.

Read [Time tracking](/help/workspace/time-tracking) for how it all fits together.
